# Promorg 1.3.0

Draft notes for the GitHub release `v1.3.0`. Paste everything below the line into the release description.

---

**Promorg 1.3.0: make it look the way you like (themes, your own colours, matte or gloss, text size and more), make your own promos, and listen through the tracks sent to you.**

**Install:** download `Promorg-1.3.0-Setup.exe` and run it, or use `Promorg-1.3.0-Standalone.exe`, which needs no
install. If the .NET 10 Desktop Runtime isn't on the PC, the installer offers to download and install it from
Microsoft. Neither file is code-signed, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you: click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Updating**
- **From Promorg 1.0.0 or 0.5.4:** **Settings › About & updates › Check for updates**, then **Download and
  install**. (Installed for all users of the PC? Download the installer and run it yourself: Windows asks for an
  administrator.) The update is checked against its release signature before it runs. Your mail, releases and settings
  are kept.
- **From Promo Organiser 0.2.0:** the same, in Promo Organiser. Your mail, releases, settings and saved passwords
  move over to Promorg on its first start.
  - **Close Promo Organiser before running the Standalone** (or the installer, if you run it by hand). If it's still
    open, Promorg asks you to close it first. If you can't see its window, end PromoOrganiser.exe in Task Manager
    (Ctrl+Shift+Esc).
  - **Re-pin Promorg on the taskbar.** A pin made for Promo Organiser points at the old program, which the update
    removes.
  - **Don't go back to Promo Organiser 0.2.0** after Promorg's first start: your data has moved, so it would open empty.
- **The database upgrade is one-way.** Once 1.3.0 has started, an older Promorg can't open your data: if you
  try the 1.3.0 Standalone first, your installed copy then needs updating too (and the other way round). An older
  copy now says so clearly, with the way back. 1.3.0 keeps a copy of your database from before the upgrade,
  `promorg.db.before-schema-10.<date>.bak`, beside it (see Back up and restore in the README).
- **A mailbox on a mail server Promorg doesn't list** (Other, in Settings › Mailboxes): type its password once
  more after updating (Settings › Mailboxes › Edit…). Saved passwords are now tied to the server they were saved
  for, and one saved by an older version can't say which that was. Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, AOL, Fastmail and Zoho
  mailboxes carry on without asking.

What came in 1.0 (cards, your accent colour, the heading row, the details pane's new layout) is in the
[1.0.0 release](https://github.com/geodnb/promorg-releases/releases/tag/v1.0.0).

**New in 1.3.0: the look is yours**

- **The appearance button** (a half-filled circle) at the top right of the window, and at the foot of the left
  rail, or **Ctrl+Shift+L**: pick a theme, the text size, the spacing and the finish. Each change shows as you
  make it.
- **15 themes.** Match Windows, Light and Dark; Black, Sepia and Dusk; colourful dark ones, **Midnight**,
  **Forest**, **Plum** and **Studio**; colourful light ones, **Mint**, **Sky** and **Rose**; and High contrast light
  and dark. Every theme is checked so text stays readable, on every button, tab and selected row.
- **Finish: Matte, Soft or Gloss.** Matte is flat with no shadows; Soft is the look you know; Gloss gives buttons,
  tabs and the rail a glossy sheen with deeper shadows.
- **Your own themes.** Settings › Appearance › **New theme from the current one…**: pick the window, card, text and
  accent colours (and the status and mailbox colours if you like), with a live preview. Promorg keeps text
  readable and tells you when it adjusted a colour, with **Use my colour anyway**. Share a theme as a small file
  with **Export…** and **Import…**. Putting one on asks **Keep this look?** and goes back by itself after 15 seconds
  if you don't answer.
- **Accent colour:** Windows' own, the theme's, or one of eight.
- **Text size** from 85% to 175% (**Ctrl+Plus**, **Ctrl+Minus**, **Ctrl+0**), on top of Windows' own text size.
  **Spacing:** Compact, Standard or Roomy (buttons and rows at least 44 pixels high). **Font:** Windows' own,
  Verdana, Atkinson Hyperlegible (built in, made to be easy to read), or one installed on your PC.
- **Statuses have shapes as well as colours:** ● New, ◐ Reviewed, ◆ Shortlisted, ■ Scheduled, ✓ Uploaded and
  ✕ Rejected (in Tracks: ● New, ◐ Maybe, ✓ Accepted, ✕ Rejected), and there's a colour-blind-safe set of status
  colours.
- **Easier to see:** a stronger focus outline, less motion, underlined links, and notices that stay longer or until
  you close them.
- **Settings › Appearance** has it all in one place, with a preview and a reset beside anything you've changed.
  Your look is saved for you on this PC, not in the database, and **Back up now** saves it with each backup
  (**Restore the look…** puts it back).
- **If a look goes wrong:** hold **Shift** while Promorg starts for the standard look, or press **Ctrl+Shift+F12**
  and then Enter in any window to get readable colours back.
- **Settings** is also at the top right now, beside the appearance button and Help.
- **A bigger window:** Promorg opens at most of your screen (never smaller than before).
- **Create** and **Tracks** have a small music note on their icons.
- **Closing asks first:** "Close Promorg?", with **Back up first** (it says when the last backup was made) and
  **Don't ask again**. Settings › Maintenance can turn the question back on, or back up every time. Nothing is
  asked when Windows shuts down or an update closes Promorg.
- **Sender checks read mail services' results the way the standard says:** parts a service adds that the standard
  doesn't define (Fastmail adds some) are ignored, so genuine promos aren't taken for forgeries. A results line that
  can't be read at all is a possible forgery for a promo platform's email (it goes to Other, saying why). An email
  your rule puts in Promos is marked ★ unless your mail service's checks verified its sender ("couldn't confirm the
  sender" when nothing passed for a domain with no DMARC policy, "couldn't check the sender" when the checks gave
  nothing to go on), and its link asks before it opens. A result smuggled into the results
  by a crafted sender address or server name isn't believed: a DKIM pass counts only with the email's own signature
  behind it, and the sender address in the results must be the Return-Path the mail service recorded. An email can
  no longer get round the limit on how many parts Promorg will read.

**Also new since 1.0** (from 1.2.1, which was never published and is folded into this release)

- **Tracks**, a new view for the music sent to you (below). Tabs show **All tracks**, just the **Submissions** (the
  tracks sent to you by email) or just what's **On this PC**, and the status tabs have colours: **New** orange,
  **Maybe** yellow, **Accepted** green, **Rejected** red. It now looks through all your email, newest first, and
  says how many emails it still has to look at.
- **Clearer status colours in Promos.** **New** orange, **Scheduled** yellow and **Rejected** red are brighter;
  every status tab has a light tint of its colour, and the selected one a strong tint with a thicker outline.
- **Restore a backup…** (Settings › Maintenance): pick a backup; Promorg checks it, keeps your current data, and
  puts the backup back the next time it starts.
- **Delete with its mail…** (Settings › Mailboxes): removes a mailbox and everything Promorg keeps from it.
- **A log** (Settings › Maintenance › **Open the log**): what Promorg did and any errors, to help when something
  goes wrong. No mail text, addresses or passwords go in it.
- **Promos:** while you type a release date, the details pane shows how it was read (and the other way round
  when "05/06" could be either). A release expected "some time in November" stays **not out yet** until the
  month is over. A date due within a week is bold as well as amber.
- **Create a Promo:** with several addresses, emails go as **Bcc** so no one sees the others (you can turn it
  off). The "out now" email says "Out on 15 January" before the day. The demo email names the right kind of
  release (EP, album, single…) and only says "unsigned" when it is. **What it still needs** checks that links are
  web links, the artwork can be shown, and the date hasn't passed.
- **Updates** are checked once a day while Promorg is open (when Check at start is on), and Settings shows when.
- **Uninstalling** says what's kept (your mail database, backups and saved passwords) and how to remove them,
  and asks you to close Promorg first if it's open.
- **Keyboard and screen readers:** Tab moves sensibly through Setlist, the picker and the Settings lists; the
  keyboard stays in the list after **Mark handled** and goes into the view after Alt+1 to Alt+8; empty cells,
  icons and rows are read out properly; messages in Settings, Create a Promo and Tracks are announced; selected
  tabs keep the normal text colour with a stronger outline.
- **Screens:** on a short screen the left rail shows icons only, and the window opens on, and fits, the monitor
  it's on.

**Also fixed since 1.0** (from 1.2.1's full independent review, and a check of its fixes)
- **Saved passwords** are only ever sent to the server they were saved for, even if the database is changed.
- **Damaged data:** Promorg checks the database when it starts, before a backup and before an upgrade. If it's
  damaged, it says so and offers to restore a backup, instead of carrying on.
- **Senders that can't be verified:** a promo from a platform whose sender couldn't be checked is marked ★, kept
  apart from releases a verified email speaks for, and Promorg asks before opening its link. On a mail server
  Promorg doesn't list, the check is weaker, so senders there are never shown as verified.
- **Your sender rules:** a label you've set a rule for still lands in Promos when its mailing service fails a
  "report only" check (marked ★); only mail the label's own domain says to refuse goes to Other.
- **Faulty or hostile mail servers:** time limits on signing out, limits on how much a server can send, a guard
  so a mailbox that made Promorg close is checked carefully next time, and an email that can't be stored is
  skipped instead of stopping the sync. The mail library is updated, so an email nested thousands of levels deep
  can no longer crash Promorg.
- **Download from mailbox…** finds the email by its Message-ID, so never someone else's email that has taken its
  number, and refuses a file that arrives shorter than the email says.
- **Files sent to you:** attachment names are made safe for Windows, and only music files are played, opened in
  your player or saved; **Link a file…** offers music files only.
- **Label spellings** asks before renaming stored releases.
- **A busy database:** Promorg waits a moment, then says so plainly; your typing is kept and saved later.
- **Tracks:** closing Promorg during a download waits for it properly; the reply account you picked is kept; the
  right pane is saved.
- **Settings › Mailboxes** shows each mailbox's server.

**Also new since 1.0** (from 1.2.0, which was never published and is folded into this release)

- **Create a Promo.** A new view on the left for your own releases, to send to a distributor, a promo service, a
  store such as Bandcamp, or a label as a demo.
  - **Release**: title, artist, label, catalogue number, UPC/EAN, release date, genre, ℗ and © lines, the promo
    text and the artwork (Promorg keeps a copy and tells you its size).
  - **Tracks**: mix, artists, remixers, ISRC, BPM, key, length, explicit, songwriters, producers, file name.
  - **Artist**: a profile saved once for all the artist's promos: Spotify and Apple Music IDs (paste the artist's
    link), store and social links, business email, bio, press photos. Music-related details only: nothing personal.
  - **Files and sending**: where the files are kept (Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, SoundCloud, WeTransfer…)
    and where it's going (Triple Vision, Label Worx, DistroKid, Inflyte, FATdrop, Bandcamp and more, or **Your
    services…** to add your own). **What it still needs** lists what that kind of place asks for: a distributor
    wants every code and credit and 3000 × 3000 artwork, a label demo little more than a listening link.
    **Copy all details** or **Save details…** (text, or a CSV with a row a track).
  - **Emails**: drafts made from the promo: a promo to DJs and channels, a demo to a label, a release to a
    distributor, a pitch to playlists, blogs or press, an "out now" and a follow-up. Change anything; then **Open
    in Gmail**, **Open in email app**, copy, or save as an email file.
  - **Notes** on each promo and each artist.
- **Tracks.** Your submissions, the tracks sent to you for your channel or label, and music on your PC, in one
  list.
  - Found in **Direct** and **Other**: a music file attached, or a SoundCloud, Dropbox, WeTransfer or similar link
    in an email about music. Newsletters and machine-sent mail are left out.
  - **Scan this PC…** says exactly what it will do before it starts: it looks in your folders (or every drive) for
    music of a minute or more, skips Windows', programs' and games' folders and short sounds, reads only names,
    sizes and tags, and adds only the folders you tick. Or **Add folder…** and **Add files…**.
  - A track too big to keep with its email (over 5 MB) is listed by name: **Download from mailbox…** fetches just
    that file when you ask (read-only, as a sync connects; the email isn't marked as read), or download it yourself
    and add it, and it joins its submission.
  - **A player**: play through the list, with previous, next, position and volume. See what the email says and what
    the file's own tags say (length, format, BPM, key, genre, label…), and keep your notes.
  - **Accept**, **Maybe** or **Reject**. Accepting drafts a reply saying you like it and asking for more music;
    rejecting drafts a polite no with your feedback. **Reply templates…** sets your channel or label's name and both
    emails. Open the reply in Gmail (from the mailbox it came to) or your email app, copy it, or save it.
  - **Export playlist…** saves the tracks on your PC as an .m3u8.
- **Notes on contacts.** Keep your own notes on each artist, label, distributor, platform or sender in Contacts;
  they show in the list and the search finds them.

Promorg still never sends email, signs in to music services or uploads anything: it opens their websites and your
email for you.

**Also new since 1.0** (from 1.1.0, which was never published and is folded into this release)

- **The counts on the left show what's new.** The numbers on Promos, Messages, Direct and Other now count what
  arrived since you last opened that view, so opening it clears its count. After updating they start empty.
  - **Right-click a view** on the left for **Clear count**, or, in Messages, Direct and Other, **Mark all as
    handled** (**Dismiss all**), which asks first and covers the mailbox tab you're on.
  - **Settings › Appearance › Counts on the left** can make them count everything not dealt with yet, as
    before, or turn them off.
- **Several emails at once.** In Messages, Direct and Other, Ctrl+click or Shift+click to select several emails
  (Ctrl+A for all), and **Mark handled** (**Dismiss**) marks them all. **Mark all shown handled** is now a button.
- **Open in Gmail.** Select an email in Messages, Direct or Other and click **Open in Gmail** (or right-click it)
  to open that email itself in your browser, in the right Google account: for its attachments, to reply, or to see
  it as it was sent. Yahoo, iCloud, AOL, Fastmail and Zoho mailboxes open their webmail.
- **Open link opens the email's link.** In Messages and Direct, when an email has no promo link (an artist sending
  their mailchi.mp page, say), **Open link** opens its first web link, and **Create release from this** gives that
  link to the release. Enter or a double-click opens a promo link, or else reads the email in its own window.
- **Calmer selected tabs.** A selected tab is a soft wash with an outline instead of a solid block of colour: a status
  tab in its status's colour (below); a mailbox tab, and the tabs in Settings, in your Windows accent colour, with a
  mailbox tab a plain lighter grey in dark mode. In a Windows contrast theme they use your Highlight colour.
- **Contacts.** A new view on the left: the artists, labels, distributors, promo platforms and email senders in your
  promos, each kind on its own tab, with how many promos (and how many still New), when the latest arrived, their
  email addresses and their promos. **Show in Promos** shows just that contact's promos.
- **Pick the track.** In a setlist's **Track** column, pick which track you'll play from each promo: the list offers
  the tracks in the promo's emails (its tracklist), or type your own. It's what the tracklist and playlist use.
- **Setlists as playlists.** Setlist › **Export…** saves a playlist for DJ software (.m3u8, offered first) that
  rekordbox, Serato, Traktor, Engine DJ and VirtualDJ can import, a tracklist ("Artist - Track", one per line), or a
  CSV file. For the playlist, pick the folder where you keep the promos' audio files: Promorg finds each track by its
  file name and tells you which it couldn't find.
- **A lighter look.** Buttons are slightly raised, sections have a soft shadow instead of a hard outline, and lists
  have no lines between rows: the row under the pointer or selected is a soft rounded band.
- **Status colours.** Each status tab has its own colour, and the selected one is tinted in it: **Rejected** red,
  **Uploaded** green, **New** orange, **Reviewed** light blue, **Shortlisted** purple and **Scheduled** yellow.
- **"Listened" is now "Reviewed".** Same status, a clearer name (still Ctrl+2); your releases keep their status.
- **Check for a newer SQLite.** Settings › About & updates shows the SQLite version Promorg keeps your data with, and
  can ask sqlite.org whether a newer one is out. SQLite is built into Promorg, so a newer one arrives with a Promorg
  update. This release has SQLite 3.53.3, the newest packaged for .NET so far.
- **Received and Mailbox together.** With several mailboxes, the Promos list now shows both the received date and
  the Mailbox column in a normal-sized window (about 1500 px wide or more). In a narrower window it hides Platform
  first, then the received date on the All mailboxes tab, or the Mailbox column on a mailbox's own tab.

**Also fixed since 1.0** (from 1.2.0's full independent review)
- **Updates:** the installer that runs is always the one whose signature was checked, even if another program tries to
  swap its folder at the last moment; a copy installed for all users is updated by running the installer
  yourself, so nothing Promorg starts runs as administrator.
- **Mail in older character sets** (Outlook's windows-1252 with curly apostrophes or a euro sign, Cyrillic, Japanese,
  Chinese, Korean) is read with the right characters, and such a label mailout is a promo again. Stored mail is re-read
  once after the update.
- **An email made of very many links** can no longer stall a sync or freeze the window.
- **Typing is never lost:** pressing Enter in Add promo or the mailbox form, or a shortcut such as Alt+1 to Alt+8 or
  Alt+N, saves what you were typing first.
- **Promos:** **It's a promo** asks about unsaved changes first; every way of opening a ★ release asks before opening
  its link; a status set in the details pane clears the ● mark; the list keeps the keyboard after a change.
- **Setlist** no longer rebuilds itself while you're typing a track or note during a sync.
- **Small screens:** the left rail fits a 1080p laptop at 150% (or scrolls), the window opens fully on screen, and tall
  dialogs scroll.
- **Tracks** copies an emailed track and reads its tags only when you play it, save it or open it in your player; a broken audio file can't
  stop a scan or keep Promorg from closing or starting.
- **Backups** are checked before they count, and **Help › Backing up and restoring** says how to restore one.
- **Licences:** Settings › About & updates › **Third-party notices** lists the open-source software Promorg includes
  (and, from 1.3.0, the Atkinson Hyperlegible font's licence).

**Fixed since 1.0** (from 1.1.0)
- Removing a release no longer hides another label's promo that uses the same short catalogue number ("EP01").
- A cover you downloaded is kept when you click **It's a promo**.
- **Mark handled** / **Dismiss** says **Mark not handled** / **Undo dismiss** on an email that's already handled, so
  a second press doesn't quietly bring it back.
- Several selected emails stay selected while a sync refreshes the list.
- If you change a mailbox's server or login, Promorg asks for the password again rather than sending the saved one
  to the new server.
- Dark theme: no white block in Setlist before you pick a setlist, and the email text no longer sits in a grey box.
- Settings has a **Close** button (Esc), and a few labels read better ("Move to Promos", "1 promo").

**Known issues**
- Starting Promorg a second time sometimes says "Promorg is already running…" even though the first copy is fine.
  This can happen when it's started from a launcher or script, or while the first copy is still starting. Switch to
  the Promorg window that's already open.
- If a backup or security program briefly holds Promorg's lock file, Promorg may also say it's already running when
  it isn't. Try again in a moment.
- The first start after updating to 1.3.0 re-reads your stored mail once, in the background (so mail in older
  character sets shows correctly, and sender checks are made again). A large mailbox, or many Promo-Cloud or Promoly emails, makes it slower.
- Promo-Cloud tracklists that name versions in unusual ways can give a wrong artist, or none. Examples are
  "Sunrise Run - Kay Vee Edit", or a track that's just called "Original". An artist worked out from a tracklist is
  always marked ⚠ to check: correct it, or click **They're right**.

**Files**
- `Promorg-1.3.0-Setup.exe`: the installer.
- `Promorg-1.3.0-Standalone.exe`: the standalone app (no install, .NET included).
- `.sha256`: each file's SHA-256, for checking by hand.
- `.sig`: each file's release signature, which the app checks before installing an update.
- `PromoOrganiser-1.3.0-Setup.exe` and its `.sig`: the same installer under the old name, only so that Promo
  Organiser 0.2.0 finds the update by itself. Download the `Promorg-` files.
